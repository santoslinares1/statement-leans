## Quality flags

### Receivables growing faster than sales (2022–2023)

Severity: high

Accounts receivable increased faster than revenue, which can mean sales quality is weaker than the top line suggests (channel stuffing, looser credit, or slower collections).

Receivables grew 38.9% vs revenue 2.9% from 2022 to 2023.

### Inventory growing faster than cost of sales (2022–2023)

Severity: high

Inventory grew faster than COGS, which can point to slowing demand, obsolete stock, or production that is outrunning sales.

Inventory grew 33.3% vs COGS 6.2% from 2022 to 2023.

### Working capital deteriorated (2022–2023)

Severity: medium

Current assets minus current liabilities declined over the latest year, which can signal tighter liquidity or a heavier short-term funding need.

Working capital fell from 180 to 70 (change -110) between 2022 and 2023.

### Interest coverage weakened (2022–2023)

Severity: medium

EBIT covers interest expense by a smaller multiple than a year earlier. That leaves less room if earnings fall or borrowing costs rise.

Interest coverage moved from 5.71x to 3.50x between 2022 and 2023.

### Earnings outpaced operating cash flow (2023)

Severity: high

Net income is well above operating cash flow, so a larger share of reported profit is coming from accruals rather than cash generated by the business.

In 2023, operating cash flow / net income was 0.31 (flag if below 0.80 while net income is positive).
